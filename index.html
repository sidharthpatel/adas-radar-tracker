Radar Track

A Kalman filter tracking vehicles from noisy radar-style detections on a highway — live, in 3D.

Every radar, in every car with adaptive cruise control or automatic emergency braking, has this exact problem: it gets a burst of noisy position readings many times a second and has to answer "where is that car, really, and how fast is it going?" A Kalman filter is the standard answer — it fuses a physics prediction ("it was probably still moving the same way") with each new noisy reading, weighing the two by how confident it currently is. The red dots below are the raw, noisy detections; the green boxes are the filter's cleaned-up estimate of where each vehicle actually is.

How it works

Each track keeps a state [x, y, vx, vy] and repeats two steps every frame:

Predict — move the estimate forward using its last known velocity, and grow its uncertainty a little (the world could have changed).

Update — when a new noisy detection arrives, pull the estimate toward it, weighted by how much to trust the sensor versus the prediction (the Kalman gain).

Which detection belongs to which track is its own problem — this demo uses simple nearest-neighbor matching, spawning a new track for anything unmatched and dropping tracks that go unseen too long.
